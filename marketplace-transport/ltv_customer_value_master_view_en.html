Customer value and list priorities — GoGoX HK Platform on-demand

All figures queried from BigQuery on 2026-08-03. Customers: 936,648 Platform on-demand shippers (business_segment = 'PlatformOD'), HK van and truck jobs only. Ranking date 2026-03-31. Result period 2026-04-01 to 2026-06-30 (91 days), which the ranking never saw. Two BigQuery headers used throughout: platform_take = what GoGoX keeps (driver_commission + premium_platform_support); gtv = gross_transaction_value, what the customer pays. Every amount on this page is platform_take, never gtv — gtv is roughly 7.4 times larger, so mixing the two changes every conclusion.

The top 10% of customers bring in 69% of the money.
The other 90% together bring in 31%. In eight of the ten groups, more than half the customers never ordered again in the next three months. So spreading budget evenly across customers wastes most of it.

Three findings

  • Money is very concentrated. The best 10% of customers brought in 68.8% of platform revenue over the next 91 days. The bottom 10% brought in 1.0%. Per customer that is HK$158.7 against HK$2.3 — a 69x gap.
  • The gap is about coming back, not about spending more per job. Every group pays roughly HK$20–22 per job. What changes is how often they order: 7.5 jobs per customer in the top group against 0.1 in the bottom. Value follows frequency, not price.
  • Our best customers do not need discounts. 92.8% of the top 500 ordered again in the next 91 days on their own. A discount sent to them pays for business that was going to happen anyway.

Three recommendations

  • Give the top 500 service, not discounts. Named account owner, priority matching, faster support. Move their discount budget to the groups that actually need a reason to come back.
  • Set the campaign pool at customers ranked 2,501 to 10,000. They still bring HK$330–480 each over 91 days, and 15–18% of them do not come back on their own — so a nudge can change the outcome.
  • Do not run one-by-one campaigns below rank 50,000. Half of that group earns us nothing in a quarter. Reach them with zero-cost channels only, and keep them off the paid list.
One limit to state up front. This page ranks customers and that ranking is reliable. It does not promise a dollar amount for any single customer — per-customer error is still high (see Accuracy and coverage). Use it to decide who to spend on, not how much each person is worth.

How to read the last row. "All customers" is the whole customer base: 936,648 people. The HK$495 in that row is an average per customer, not a sum — the whole base is worth about HK$464 million over 120 months. Ten groups × about 93,665 people each = 936,648.
"Running total" counts customer groups, not months. Work on the top group only and you reach 68.8% of the value. Add the second group and you reach 80.9%. Keep going down all ten and you reach 100%. It answers one question: how far down the list do we need to go?
None of the 120 months has happened yet. We measured three months of real money (2026-04-01 to 2026-06-30), turned that into a yearly figure, then assumed ten more years of it, shrinking 15% a year. So the lifetime column is a forecast of the future, not money already collected. Nothing in it has been earned yet.

‡ The lifetime columns are arithmetic, not model output. Revenue per year = revenue per customer over 91 days × 365 ÷ 91. Contribution = revenue per year × 5.354 — that is 120 months, with customers declining 15% a year (measured from a real group of 372,405 customers), no discounting. The discounted version is deliberately not shown here: the discount rate is a Finance policy, and changing it moves only that one column while Contribution stays the same — so the business side does not need to wait for Finance. It is kept in Lifetime value scenarios below. Only the first 30 months have real data; the remaining 90 months are projected from the measured decline rate. See Lifetime value scenarios below for the conservative and optimistic versions. Share of lifetime value equals each group's share of 91-day revenue, because the lifetime figure is a fixed multiple of it.

Where HK$3,405 comes from. The lifetime column is not measured over 120 months — it is worked out from three months of real money. The full working is in ④ Customer value over time below.
  • Top two groups = 80.9% of revenue. Everything below P90 adds up to less than a fifth.
  • Middle customer earns HK$0.00 in nine of the ten groups. The averages are carried by a minority who keep ordering. Reporting only averages would overstate how healthy these groups are.
  • The still-active score drops from 0.86 to 0.00. Top-group customers were ordering right up to the ranking date. Bottom-group customers ordered once and never returned.
  • Top-group customers have also been with us longest — 705 days against 410. They are not just busier, they are more loyal.

Coverage at a glance. A list of 2,500 customers covers 17% of the next quarter's revenue. 10,000 customers covers 30%. 50,000 customers covers 57%.

From three months of real money to a 120-month figure

Three steps, using the most valuable group (P100) as the example. Nothing is hidden:

StepWorkingResultIs it real money?
1. Money actually collected91 days, 2026-04-01 to 2026-06-30HK$158.7 per customerYes — counted, not estimated
2. Turned into one yearHK$158.7 × 365 ÷ 91HK$636 per yearArithmetic on real money
3. Stretched over 120 monthsHK$636 × 5.354  (ten years, each year 15% smaller than the last)HK$3,405No — a projection

The multiplier 5.354 is the sum of ten years shrinking 15% a year (1 + 0.85 + 0.85² + … + 0.85⁹). The 15% comes from the chart above, measured on 372,405 real customers (queried 2026-08-03, first order 2024-01-01 to 2024-06-30, tracked to 2026-06-30). Applying the same three steps to the whole customer base gives HK$495 per customer, or about HK$464 million in total.

Step 3 is where the certainty stops. Steps 1 and 2 are money we have counted. Step 3 assumes the next ten years look like the last three months, only shrinking steadily. That is why the lifetime column is marked ‡ and why we quote a range, not a single promise.
Why this chart matters for a lifetime number. Two years of history is not a lifetime. Because the line is flat rather than falling to zero, the part after month 24 has to be estimated rather than ignored — and any lifetime figure we quote must say which decline rate was assumed. See Lifetime value scenarios below.

⑥ Ranking with and without the model

Sorting customers by past earnings already works well. The model does better.

Both methods pick a "best 10%" list. We then check how much of the next 91 days' revenue that list actually captured. Picking customers at random would capture 10%.

The model captures 6.2 percentage points more than plain sorting, on exactly the same 935,992 customers. That is the reason to run a model at all — but note the simple sort already gets most of the way, so the simple version is a safe fallback if the model ever breaks.

Source: reporting.pres_ltv_forecast_vs_actual_summary and analytics.mart_ltv_customer_tier_baseline (queried 2026-08-03, PlatformOD, ranking date 2026-03-31, result period 2026-04-01 to 2026-06-30, 935,992 customers).

The advantage grows once the biggest customers are removed. Drop the top 1% and the gap widens to 8.7 points; drop the top 5% and it widens to 11.4 points. So the model is not just recognising a handful of whales — it ranks ordinary customers better too.
